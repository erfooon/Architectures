Clean Architecture gives clear boundaries.
Vertical Slice Architecture gives clear features.

Together, the codebase becomes easier to navigate because everything is grouped by what the app does, not only by technical type.

𝟏) 𝐃𝐨𝐦𝐚𝐢𝐧 

This is the business core. No EF Core. No web. No external dependencies.

📁 Domain
├── 📁 Constants
├── 📁 Units
│  ├── Unit.cs
│  ├── IUnitRepository.cs
│  ├── UnitCreatedDomainEvent.cs
│  └── UnitErrors.cs
└── 📁 UnitGroups

𝟐) 𝐀𝐩𝐩𝐥𝐢𝐜𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧

Instead of “Services” folders, everything is grouped by feature.

📁 Application
├── 📁 Abstractions
├── 📁 Behaviors
├── 📁 Exceptions
├── 📁 Messaging
│  ├── DomainEventHandler.cs
│  ├── IDomainEventHandler.cs
│  └── IRequestHandler.cs
├── 📁 Units
│  ├── 📁 CreateUnit
│  │  ├── CreateUnitCommand.cs
│  │  ├── CreateUnitCommandHandler.cs
│  │  └── CreateUnitCommandValidator.cs
│  ├── 📁 UpdateUnit
│  ├── 📁 DeleteUnit
│  ├── 📁 GetUnit
│  └── 📁 GetUnits
└── 📁 UnitGroups

✅ Each slice contains everything for that use case: Request → Validation → Handler → Response.

𝟑) 𝐈𝐧𝐟𝐫𝐚𝐬𝐭𝐫𝐮𝐜𝐭𝐮𝐫𝐞 

This layer handles how things actually work.
Databases, EF Core, messaging, and external services live here.

📁 Infrastructure
├── 📁 Database
│  ├── 📁 Migrations
│  └── ApplicationDbContext.cs
├── 📁 Units
│  ├── UnitConfiguration.cs
│  └── UnitRepository.cs
├── 📁 UnitGroups
├── 📁 Messaging
└── DependencyInjection.cs

𝟒) 𝐏𝐫𝐞𝐬𝐞𝐧𝐭𝐚𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧

This layer is responsible for receiving requests and returning responses.
It should stay thin and contain no business logic.

📁 Presentation
├── 📁 Units
│  ├── CreateUnit.cs
│  ├── UpdateUnit.cs
│  ├── DeleteUnit.cs
│  ├── GetUnit.cs
│  └── GetUnits.cs
├── 📁 UnitGroups
├── 📁 Middlewares
└── Program.cs
